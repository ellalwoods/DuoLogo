# On the job: what clerks, paralegals and interns actually do

A working guide for a law student or paralegal starting at a large Sydney firm (for example, Allens) in a clerk, paralegal or intern role, with a focus on IP work. Read it before your first day. It covers the kinds of task you're likely to be given, and the habits that make you useful.

**How to read the sources.** Each section says where its facts come from. Some Allens pages could not be read while this was written, so Allens-specific points come from search summaries of the firm's own pages. **Check them against the Allens graduate careers site before you rely on them.** Practical habits are general good practice, not quotes from a firm.

---

## 1. Your role in one line

You are the support for a lawyer's matter: you do the research, drafting, document work and admin that lets them advise the client, and you learn by doing it well.

**What the sources say**
- Allens' clerkship is a **10-week summer program** over the December/January holidays, with **two rotations** in different practice groups, on **real matters**. Eligibility is penultimate or final year of law. *(Search summary of Allens' clerkship pages; check the current listing.)*
- Allens' FAQ says clerks' day-to-day work is similar to a junior lawyer's, including court hearings and client meetings. *(Search summary of the Allens FAQ.)*
- Clerks and paralegals at other Sydney and Australian firms are described as doing research, drafting, court documents and correspondence, attending meetings, and file review. *(Job listings and firm pages, below.)*

## 2. The kinds of task you'll get

Grouped by what you're doing, not by job title. Most matters involve several of these at once.

### A. Research
- **Research memos** on a point of law: find the Act, the sections, the leading cases and any recent developments, then summarise what they mean for the client.
  *Source: Clayton Utz and Maddocks clerkship listings ("engage in research, draft client memos").*
- **Summaries of documents or cases** for the supervising lawyer, often with a chronology.
  *Source: McInnes Wilson tax clerk listing ("research summaries, chronologies and reports").*

### B. Drafting
- **Correspondence** to clients, other side's lawyers or the court.
  *Source: Carroll & O'Dea Sydney clerk listing ("preparing correspondence, court documents, statements…").*
- **Court documents**: affidavits, statements, subpoenas, filing.
  *Source: Carroll & O'Dea Sydney and Parramatta listings.*
- **Advice drafts and memos** to clients, reviewed by the lawyer before anything goes out.
  *Source: Clayton Utz and Maddocks listings (client memos); Allens graduate review summary ("drafting advice").*

### C. Document review and due diligence
- **Reviewing files and documents** to pull out key facts, dates and risks.
  *Source: Macpherson Kelley wills clerk listing ("review file materials to identify key information and risks").*
- **Due diligence** on a transaction or matter: checking searches, title, registers and contracts for problems.
  *Source: Castle Group and McCullough Robertson paralegal listings; Mills Oakley lists IP audits and due diligence as IP services.*
- **Registers and searches.** In IP: trade mark searches, checking who owns a registration, checking renewal and non-use. *Source: LegalVision trade mark paralegal listing ("trade mark searches… preparing and filing trade mark applications"); CBP trade mark services (searches and watches).*

### D. Meetings and client contact
- **Attending client and court meetings**, usually taking notes.
  *Source: Ashurst clerk listing ("take notes during meetings… attend court"); Maddocks and Clayton Utz listings.*
- **Liaising** between the client, the other side and the team, then passing actions to the right person.
  *Source: Allens graduate review summary ("liaising with stakeholders").*

### E. Admin that keeps the matter moving
- **File management**: naming, version control, indexing, keeping the bundle current.
  *Source: McInnes Wilson clerk listing ("file management").*
- **Diary and scheduling**, booking meetings and tracking deadlines.
  *Source: Carroll & O'Dea Sydney clerk listing ("diary and scheduling support").*
- **Filing and lodgement**: online lodgement systems, court and registry filings.
  *Source: Carroll & O'Dea Parramatta listing (court filings); for IP, IP Australia lodgement.*

## 3. How to approach each kind of task

### Research
1. **Start with the question, in writing.** Write one sentence: what the lawyer needs to know and why. Ask if it's unclear.
2. **Go to primary sources first.** Legislation on legislation.gov.au, the judgment itself, then commentary. Don't cite a secondary summary as if it were the law.
3. **Check currency.** Confirm the Act or regulation is the current compilation, and check for amendments.
4. **Note what you couldn't confirm.** A short "I couldn't open the judgment; this is from [source]" saves the lawyer from relying on it by accident. This course uses the same habit (⚑).
5. **Answer first, then the workings.** Lead with the short answer, then the sections and cases behind it.

### Drafting
1. **Get the template and the house style** from the team. Don't invent a format.
2. **Draft to the reader.** A client needs the answer and the risk in plain English. The other side's lawyer needs precise facts.
3. **Don't add facts you haven't checked.** Every date, name and figure should trace back to a document on the file.
4. **Mark your open points.** Use square brackets like [CONFIRM WITH CLIENT] so nothing unfinished goes out.
5. **Expect edits.** Read the lawyer's changes and ask about any you don't understand. That's the fastest way to learn their standard.

### Document review and due diligence
1. **Agree the scope and the issues list** with the lawyer before you start: what you're looking for, and what counts as a red flag.
2. **Make a coding or tracking sheet** (document, date, party, issue, risk, page reference). Consistency matters more than speed.
3. **Keep a note of what you reviewed and what you didn't.** Missing a document is a risk the lawyer needs to know about.
4. **Escalate early.** A serious problem (a change-of-control clause, an unregistered mark, a missing assignment) goes to the supervising lawyer the same day, not in a summary at the end.

### Meetings
1. **Prepare.** Read the file and the agenda, and know the names.
2. **Take notes in a format you can share:** attendees, date, decisions, actions with owners and dates.
3. **Send the note within a day**, marked as a draft for the lawyer to check.

### Admin and filing
1. **Check the deadline and the time zone** the moment you get a task. Put it in the diary with a reminder a day earlier.
2. **Name and save files consistently**, the way the matter team does.
3. **Confirm a filing** with the receipt or confirmation, and save it to the file.

## 4. Habits that make you useful (and keep you out of trouble)

- **Confidentiality.** Don't discuss matters outside the team, and don't put client material on personal devices or accounts. Follow the firm's IT and privacy rules.
- **Conflicts.** Before working on a new client or matter, check you haven't been involved in a related one.
- **Privilege.** Mark advice and internal drafts as privileged and confidential, and don't forward them outside the team.
- **Time recording.** Record your time as you go, with a clear description. Good time records are how the firm bills clients fairly.
- **Ask well.** Ask one clear question with what you've already tried: "I've checked X and Y; the open point is Z. Should I go to the register or the judgment?"
- **Check your own work.** Verify every citation and section number against the primary source before you hand work up. Then say what you checked.
- **Tell people early** if a deadline is at risk. A warning on Monday is useful. A missed deadline on Friday is not.
- **Keep learning notes.** Write down terms, forms and processes you didn't know. That's the fastest way to build the knowledge the work needs.

## 5. IP-specific tasks you may see

| Task | What it involves | Starting point |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade mark search** | Searching the register and the market for similar marks in the same classes, and reporting the results | IP Australia's trade mark search |
| **Trade mark filing** | Preparing the application: the mark, the goods and services and their classes, the applicant details | Trade Marks Regulations (classes); unit 2.2 |
| **Opposition or non-use check** | Checking an earlier mark's use and renewal, and any deadlines for opposing | Trade Marks Act ss 52, 92; units 2.6–2.7 |
| **Copyright ownership check** | Who created the work, whether it was employee or commissioned work, and any assignment | Copyright Act ss 35, 196; units 1.6–1.7 |
| **Infringement letter review** | Checking the claim against the elements: use as a mark, similarity, goods, defences | Units 2.8–2.10; unit 1.13 |
| **Due diligence on an IP portfolio** | Listing each registration, owner, status, renewal date and any conflicts or licences | Registers; IP due diligence checklist from the team |
| **Overseas filings** | Checking priority dates and deadlines under the Paris Convention and Madrid | Unit 0.4; Trade Marks Act s 29 |

*Sources for the IP tasks: LegalVision trade mark paralegal listing and CBP trade mark services (search and filing duties); the Act references are from this course's checked units.*

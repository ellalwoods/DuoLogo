# Duo Logo — Concept v0.2 · Design A: The Bower

> A daily app for learning Australian intellectual property law, built around the most IP-obsessed animal in the country: the satin bowerbird.

*(A second, more scientific design is in [`DESIGN-B-LAB.md`](DESIGN-B-LAB.md). The course, lesson loop and question types are the same in both. Only the world and the look change.)*

---

## 1. The one-line pitch

**Learn who owns what in Australia, one blue treasure at a time.**

DuoVino made wine feel like wine. Duo Logo makes IP law feel like the thing it actually is: **making something, showing it off, and stopping others from taking it.** That's also a fair description of a bowerbird's whole life.

## 2. Why a bowerbird?

The satin bowerbird (east coast, Queensland to Victoria) is a near-perfect metaphor for IP law, and it's ours:

| What the bird does | What it teaches |
|---|---|
| Builds an **avenue bower** from sticks, then decorates a display court with carefully arranged **blue** objects: bottle caps, pegs, straws, feathers, flowers | You **create** things, and a creation is a collection of distinct parts, each protected in its own way |
| Prefers *blue*, so its display stands out from everyone else's | **Distinctiveness**, the heart of trade mark law |
| Raids rival bowers and **steals their best blue things** | **Infringement**: taking what someone else made |
| Is a brilliant **mimic** of other birds' calls | **Copying vs. coincidence**: substantial part, deceptive similarity |
| Chews berries into **paint** and brushes it on with a wad of bark: its own secret recipe | **Confidential information** and trade secrets |
| Puts on a **display** for a judge who is very hard to impress | The exam / court: applying the law to facts, persuasively |

It's distinctly Australian without leaning on clichés (no kangaroos in boxing gloves), and it gives us a whole world to build the design from.

## 3. Who it's for

- **You, learning a whole Australian IP law course** (LLB/JD level): every topic, the statutes, the leading cases, the policy debates, and plenty of problem-question practice.
- Depth is the point. Cards are fun to move through, but they carry real substance: tests broken into elements, cases with facts, issue, held and why they matter, the actual statutory words, and critique for essay questions. See [`SAMPLE-UNIT.md`](SAMPLE-UNIT.md).
- It doesn't replace the readings and lectures. It's where you learn, test and keep the content until the exam.

## 4. Simpler screens, deeper content

DuoVino has five tabs, two tracks, a map, a cellar and a daily glass. Duo Logo keeps the *screens* simple so the *content* can be deep:

- **Three screens** (Bower · Trail · Raids), plus the lesson flow.
- **One track**: a full course of 10 chapters, 81 units and ~175 lessons (about 23 hours), ending with a chapter on IP in practice. See [`SYLLABUS.md`](SYLLABUS.md).
- **One repeating shape** for every IP right (the *Five Sticks*, below), so the structure of the law *is* the structure of the app.
- **Six question formats**, each training a real legal skill.

## 5. The three screens

### 🪺 Bower (home)
One illustrated scene: your bower in a patch of eucalypt forest, two walls of sticks with a display court in front.

- **Sticks** = units completed. The walls grow as you learn. A finished chapter completes a section of wall.
- **Blue treasures** on the display court = the **leading cases** you've mastered. Each right has its own kind of object (see §6). Tap one to see its *case card*: facts in two lines, the rule in one, and the court.
- The light follows the time of day in Australian bush colours (grey dawn, white midday glare, ochre afternoon, blue dusk).
- Breeding season (roughly Sept–Jan) is **display season**: the bird is busier, and that's when the practice problem-question challenges run. It's November now, so we'd launch right in it.
- One big **Continue** button. That's it.

### 🌿 Trail (learn)
The syllabus as a walking track through the forest, one chapter per clearing. Each clearing shows its treasure type and how many sticks you've laid. Tap a unit to start. Checkpoints sit at the end of each clearing.

### 🪶 Raids (review)
Spaced repetition, framed as defence. Rival bowerbirds visit while you're away and **eye the treasures that are due for review**. When you open Raids:

- Each due treasure is "under threat." Answer its question correctly and you keep it (and it gets shinier, i.e. a longer review interval).
- Get it wrong and the rival **makes off with it**. It reappears on the Trail, a little faded, to win back.
- You fight back by **sending a letter of demand**. The joke and the lesson: if you send one and you're *wrong*, the app reminds you that unjustified threats are actionable (Trade Marks Act s 129, Patents Act s 128, Designs Act s 77, Copyright Act s 202). Nothing punitive, just a cheeky "careful, counsel."

No streaks. No guilt. A bower with a few gaps is still a bower.

## 6. The treasures: one object per right

Every right gets a blue object, a colour accent within the blue family, and a reason:

| Right | Treasure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundations** | Sticks | The structure everything else hangs on |
| **Copyright** | Blue **feathers** | Quills: authorship, expression |
| **Trade marks & brands** | Bottle **caps** | Literally branded objects |
| **Patents** | Clothes **pegs** | Small, clever, functional. (Bowerbirds famously steal blue pegs off Aussie clotheslines) |
| **Designs** | **Petals** | All about appearance, not function |
| **Secrets & culture** | **Paint** smears on the bower wall | The bird's own secret recipe |
| **Enforcement** | The **display** itself | Putting it all together |

## 7. The question formats

Six formats, each a legal skill rather than a quiz trick.

### 🏷 Tag the Right (issue spotting)
A small scenario drawn as a scene: *a Newtown café with a hand-drawn logo, a menu, a signature cup lid, a secret cold-brew method, a café name.* Drag blue tabs onto each thing to say which right protects it (copyright, trade mark, design, patent, confidential information, or *nothing*). Issue spotting is the first skill every IP exam tests.

### 📮 The Examiner's Stamp
You're the examiner at the registry. A proposed trade mark (fictional), design or patent claim slides in. **Press and hold to stamp** ACCEPTED or REFUSED, then pick the ground (e.g. *s 41: not capable of distinguishing*, *s 44: deceptively similar to an earlier mark*, *lacks novelty*). Satisfying, tactile, and drills the registrability tests.

### 🪵 Build the Bower (elements)
Every cause of action is a bower that needs all its sticks. Drag the right elements into the wall, in order. Passing off: *reputation → misrepresentation → damage*. Breach of confidence: *identified with specificity → quality of confidence → obligation → misuse*. Leave one out, or add a fake one, and the wall wobbles and falls.

### 🦜 Copycat?
The mimicry game. Two things side by side: two marks, two drawings, two song lyrics, two product shapes (all fictional). Is it a copy? Slide from *coincidence* to *infringing copy*, then pick why (substantial part, deceptive similarity, the informed user, independent creation). Teaches judgement, not just rules.

### ⏳ The Clock
Timelines and durations. Drag a dial to set how long a right lasts, or put events in order to work out who wins a filing race (priority dates, the 12-month Paris Convention window, the 12-month grace periods for patents and designs). Dates are where IP exams catch people out.

### 📜 Case Snap
Match a case to the principle it stands for. Win a case and it joins your display court as a treasure.

Plus plain multiple choice where that's genuinely the best fit.

### Checkpoint: The Display
Each chapter ends with a short **problem question**, the law-exam format, done with taps:

1. **Spot** the issues in a short fact pattern (tap the sentences that matter).
2. **Rule:** pick the provision or case for each issue.
3. **Apply:** choose which facts satisfy each element.
4. **Conclude:** pick the likely outcome and remedy.

The bird dances when you pass (80%). That's IRAC, trained without writing an essay on your phone.

## 8. The lesson loop

Same proven shape as DuoVino, with much deeper cards:

| Step | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Forage** | 8–12 cards, one idea each. Core text up to ~150 words, plus a **Go deeper** layer (up to ~300 words) for detail, dissents and later cases. Card types: Rule, Statute, Test, Case, Compare, Worked example, Critique, Trap. Section numbers in the margin. |
| 2 | **Build** | 6–8 questions testing only what the cards taught, mixed formats, every answer explained. |
| 3 | **Raid check** | One question from an earlier unit mixed in (interleaving). |
| 4 | **Display** | The unit's case treasure lands on your court, and the unit's tests and cases are added to **your outline**: a running exam summary you can export. |

~8 minutes a lesson, 2–3 lessons a unit. Chapter problem questions come in *tap mode* and *write mode* (type an answer, then compare it with a model answer and marking checklist).

## 9. Design language

### The look: *a field guide, annotated by a lawyer*
Picture a 19th-century natural-history plate (careful engraved lines, a soft watercolour wash, a specimen name in italic serif) that someone has gone over in the margins with **section numbers, case citations and blue pencil**. Nature on the page, law in the margins.

- **Statute margins:** every lesson card has a narrow left margin with the section number (`s 41 TMA`), exactly where it sits in a printed Act. Tap it for the actual wording.
- **Specimen labels:** cases are shown like museum specimen tags, tied on with string: *Self Care IP Holdings v Allergan Australia [2023] HCA 8*.
- **Paper:** warm, slightly fibrous off-white, like a field notebook. Darker "dusk" mode for night study.

### Colour: blue is the brand, because blue is the bird
- **Satin blue-black** `#1C1F3A`: the bird's plumage, used for ink and type.
- **Bowerbird violet** `#5A4FCF`: the bird's eye, used for primary actions.
- **Bottle-cap cobalt** `#2E6BE6`, **peg sky** `#58A6E8`, **petal periwinkle** `#8C9EF0`: one blue per right.
- **Bush neutrals**: stick tan `#C9A97A`, eucalyptus sage `#9DB0A0`, ochre `#C8743A` (used sparingly, for warnings and "REFUSED" stamps).
- **Paper** `#F6F1E6`.

Everything that belongs to *you* is blue. Everything else is bush-coloured. Your progress literally stands out, like a real bower in the scrub.

### Type
- **Serif** for headings and lesson text (a field-guide / statute-book feel), e.g. *Fraunces* or *Source Serif*.
- **Monospace** for section numbers, citations and dates, like the margin notes of an Act.

### Motion & touch
- **Stamp:** press-and-hold builds pressure, then a satisfying thunk and an ink bloom.
- **Sticks** snap into the bower wall with a small wooden click.
- A **rival bird hops in** at the edge of the screen when reviews are due.
- Passing a checkpoint: the bird does its display dance (wings flared, a treasure held in its beak).

### Voice: the dry, kind barrister
Personality in the copy, not a nagging mascot. Warm, precise, a little dry, very Australian. *"That's a deceptively similar mark. Not identical, just close enough to fool a busy shopper in a hurry, which is the only shopper the law cares about."*

An optional companion: **Peg**, the bowerbird. Peg never begs you to come back. Peg just builds, and occasionally looks pointedly at a gap in the wall.

## 10. A note on First Nations culture

One chapter covers Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property (ICIP): the carpets case, the banknote case, fake souvenir art. Given that, the app itself must practise what it teaches: **no dot-painting or other First Nations visual styles in the design**, no borrowed language for decoration, and that unit's content should be reviewed by, or drawn from, First Nations sources (e.g. Terri Janke's *True Tracks* principles). The field-guide style keeps us clearly in our own lane.

## 11. Everything is fictional except the law

All brands, logos, inventions, songs and designs in questions are **made up**. Real cases are cited by name with their real facts. That avoids putting real trade marks into a trade marks app (which would be a bit embarrassing) and lets us design examples that teach exactly one thing.

## 12. Open questions

1. **Name: decided, *Duo Logo*.** A sibling to DuoVino. The bower stays as the home screen and the bird as the theme. (One wry note for a trade marks app: like DuoVino, the name leans on Duolingo's, which is fine for a personal app but worth revisiting if it ever goes public.)
2. **Your course outline.** Sharing your unit outline or reading list would let the syllabus follow your weeks and the cases your lecturer emphasises.
3. **Same codebase as DuoVino?** The lesson engine, spaced repetition, backup and PWA shell could be reused, with the content and styles swapped. That would make it much quicker to build.
4. **Fact-checking.** Law changes. Each unit should carry a "law as at" date and get a check before it ships (DuoVino's draft-lesson flag would work for this).
5. **First slice to prototype:** Home bower + Chapter 1 (*The Map*) + one copyright unit with *Tag the Right* and *Copycat?*. That proves the look, the loop and the two most distinctive games.
